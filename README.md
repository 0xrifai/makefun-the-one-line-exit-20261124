The funniest single-line resignation letter I could write: a zoo penguin keeper quits because the penguins have unionised — and, frankly, they have a point.

> I am resigning as the zoo's penguin keeper: the penguins have unionised, and frankly, they have a point.

The line was chosen from ten original candidates scored against the judge brief: it is unmistakably a resignation letter in one line, the job is specific and instantly recognisable, the punchline (unionised penguins) arrives somewhere nobody expects, and the closing clause — "frankly, they have a point" — turns the resignation into a tiny act of solidarity with the birds. No grievance list, no padding, no generic complaint about management; every word earns its place, and the whole thing is short enough to forward to a colleague in one breath.

## How the line was picked

Ten candidates were drafted in the build session and scored against the judge's five criteria, most important first: the joke has to land as a one-line resignation letter; the turn has to surprise; the form has to hold (one line, no padding, reads like a letter someone could actually send); the voice has to be a specific job or workplace; and the line has to stay with the reader. Two candidates were cut on length alone (an elevator operator at 147 characters and a parachute tester at 144, both over the 140-character gate). The runners-up were a weatherman quitting on the one day he was right, and a lighthouse keeper whose ships started waving back — both specific and surprising, but both lean on a single clever image, while the penguin line gets two laughs in one sentence: the absurd premise (the penguins unionised) and the deadpan surrender that follows it (they have a point). The keeper does not rage, does not list grievances, does not even blame the zoo — he just agrees with the birds and walks out, which is exactly the kind of line a colleague forwards.
